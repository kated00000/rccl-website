The official kick-off meeting of the megagrant project **“Ocean Mechanisms of Eurasian Climate Variability and Change: Road to Predictability” (2026–2030)** brought together researchers from the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (MIPT), the Shirshov Institute of Oceanology of the Russian Academy of Sciences, and Fudan University (China).

Led by project leader Olga Zolina, the meeting focused on the project's scientific objectives, interdisciplinary collaboration, and research priorities for 2026.

The project aims to advance the understanding of ocean-driven mechanisms of Eurasian climate variability and change, improve regional climate predictability, and develop innovative approaches to climate modelling and forecasting.

## Key Research Priorities

During the meeting, participants discussed the following research priorities:

- Development of a consolidated database of observational and climate reanalysis products.
- Advancement of diagnostic methods for studying ocean-related climate variability.
- Application of large climate model ensembles to investigate internal climate variability.
- Development of high-resolution global and regional coupled climate modelling systems.
- Integration of artificial intelligence and machine learning methods into climate research.
- Development of data management infrastructure and optimisation of high-performance computing resources.

Special attention was given to strengthening international scientific collaboration, coordinating modelling activities, and sharing computational resources.

The meeting marked the official launch of the five-year research programme, bringing together expertise in oceanography, atmospheric science, climate dynamics, numerical modelling, and artificial intelligence to address fundamental challenges in Eurasian climate predictability.
