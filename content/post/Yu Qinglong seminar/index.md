On 8 October 2026, the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (MIPT) hosted a scientific seminar featuring a presentation by **Yu Qinglong** from Fudan University (China).

The seminar focused on recent changes in the Arctic climate system, the mechanisms of ocean–atmosphere–sea ice interactions, and their links to climate extremes in the Northern Hemisphere.

## Key Research Topics

During the seminar, Yu Qinglong presented recent research findings from his research group, highlighting the following topics:

- **Arctic amplification** — accelerated warming of the Arctic relative to the global average.
- Physical mechanisms of sea ice loss and ocean–atmosphere interactions.
- The influence of Atlantic Multidecadal Variability (AMV) and the Interdecadal Pacific Oscillation (IPO) on the Arctic climate system.
- Application of Single Model Initial-condition Large Ensembles (SMILEs) to assess internal climate variability and reduce uncertainties in climate projections.
- Links between Arctic climate change and midlatitude climate extremes, including heatwaves, droughts, and wildfires.

Particular attention was given to the mechanisms of pronounced winter warming in the Barents and Kara Seas, as well as the climatic factors contributing to the extensive Siberian wildfires in 2021.

## Scientific Discussion

The presentation was followed by a scientific discussion addressing climate signal transmission mechanisms, the role of internal climate variability, and opportunities for improving climate predictability.

The seminar contributed to the ongoing development of Russian–Chinese scientific collaboration in climate research and predictability.
